Essential documents include the original link documents tracing ownership for at least 30 years, current and historical Pahanis (ROR), Encumbrance Certificates (EC Form 15 and Form 16), revenue mutation registers, village layout or Tippani survey maps, tax assessment receipts, and layout approvals from the statutory planning authority.
Section 22-A of the Registration Act mandates a prohibition list of properties that cannot be registered. This includes government assigned lands, Wakf properties, Endowments land, acquired lands, and parcels under litigation or ceiling surplus. A property notified under Section 22-A cannot be transferred, and any transaction is void ab initio.
A standard 30-year verification for urban plotted properties typically takes 5 to 7 business days once certified copies are collated. Complex agricultural acquisitions, large commercial tracts, or 60-year extended searches involving revenue archives and field inspections require 10 to 14 business days.
A 15-year search verifies recent transactions for resale apartments with prior verified root titles. A 30-year search is the statutory benchmark that verifies continuous, uninterrupted ownership and eliminates claims under limitation laws. A 60-year search traces ancestral land back to original Inam or grant records, critical for agricultural conversions and institutional land banking.
A registered sale deed confirms the conveyance transaction, but Pahanis (Record of Rights) confirm sovereign revenue recognition, actual possession, nature of cultivation, tenancy rights, and mutation entries. Discrepancies between registered deeds and Pahanis are the primary genesis of title disputes.
